Analysis of stochastic PDEs

Research

Well-posedness and numerical analysis of stochastic equations with irregular coefficients.

Overview

My research concerns stochastic evolution equations: stochastic differential equations (SDEs) in finite dimension, and parabolic stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) such as stochastic heat equations. A typical model is

$$ dX_t = b(X_t)\,dt + dB^H_t, \qquad \partial_t u = \Delta u + b(u) + \dot W^H, $$

where the drift \(b\) may be very irregular — bounded, Hölder continuous, integrable or even a distribution — and the noise \(B^H\), \(W^H\) may be fractional, with Hurst index \(H \in (0,1)\).

Without noise, such equations can have no solution or infinitely many. The noise changes the picture: it can regularize the dynamics and restore existence and uniqueness. I study this mechanism and its consequences on three fronts — the well-posedness theory, the convergence of numerical schemes that approximate the solutions, and the behaviour of solutions and schemes over long times.

Research themes

01 Well-posedness of parabolic SPDEs and SDEs Existence, uniqueness and stability of solutions beyond the Lipschitz setting.

Proving that a stochastic equation has a unique solution, and that this solution depends continuously on the data, is the first question for any model. I study strong and pathwise well-posedness for stochastic differential equations and for parabolic stochastic partial differential equations such as stochastic heat equations, when the coefficients are too irregular for classical fixed-point arguments.

  • pathwise uniqueness
  • strong solutions
  • mild solutions
  • stochastic heat equation
02 Regularization by noise How a rough enough noise restores well-posedness for ill-posed equations.

An ordinary differential equation \(\dot x = b(x)\) with a non-Lipschitz drift may have many solutions, yet adding noise can select exactly one. This phenomenon — going back to the work of Zvonkin and Veretennikov for Brownian noise and extended to fractional noise by Catellier and Gubinelli — is a central tool in my work, both for proving uniqueness and for quantifying how much regularity the noise provides.

  • Zvonkin transform
  • averaging along paths
  • stochastic sewing
03 Singular drifts Drifts that are merely bounded, Hölder, integrable or even distributional.

Many models of interest involve drifts \(b\) with very little regularity: bounded measurable functions, functions in Hölder or Lebesgue spaces, or genuine distributions. I am interested in identifying sharp conditions on \(b\) under which the equation stays well-posed, and in understanding what happens at the threshold.

  • Hölder and Besov drifts
  • Krylov–Röckner condition
  • distributional drifts
04 Fractional noise Equations driven by fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index \(H \neq 1/2\).

Fractional Brownian motion \(B^H\) is neither a semimartingale nor a Markov process when \(H \neq 1/2\), so Itô calculus and PDE methods are no longer directly available. Its regularizing effect nevertheless improves as \(H\) decreases, which makes it a natural driver for regularization-by-noise results and for the numerical analysis of such equations.

  • fractional Brownian motion
  • Hurst parameter
  • Volterra kernels
  • local nondeterminism
05 Numerical analysis Convergence rates of discretization schemes in low-regularity regimes.

When coefficients are irregular, standard error analyses of numerical schemes break down. I study strong and weak convergence rates of time and space discretizations — the Euler–Maruyama scheme, exponential and splitting integrators, finite-difference approximations — and how the noise itself can be used to recover good rates.

  • Euler–Maruyama
  • strong and weak error
  • splitting schemes
  • convergence rates
06 Long-time behaviour Invariant measures, ergodicity and their numerical approximation.

Over long time horizons, many stochastic systems forget their initial condition and settle into a statistical equilibrium described by an invariant measure. I am interested in ergodicity for equations with irregular coefficients and in how well numerical schemes preserve, and approximate, this long-time behaviour.

  • invariant measures
  • ergodicity
  • mixing
  • approximation of invariant laws
